Privacy policy

Last updated 18 November 2026

Garagebook is run by one private person, called the owner on these pages: the person who invited you. It is a free hobby project for the owner and up to five invited riders. It is not a business, has no customers and does not make money.

Contact: ask the owner directly, the same way you received your invite link. The owner can also see your account in Garagebook's Admin control. This site has no public contact address, because Garagebook is only for invited riders.

In short. Garagebook stores only what you type into it, plus what is needed to sign you in. There are no ads, no analytics, no tracking and no cookies. Your data is not sold or shared for marketing. The database is in the European Union.

Garagebook does not ask for your email address, phone number, real name, address or date of birth. Behind the scenes each account gets a made-up placeholder address that is never used to contact you.

Only on your device

  • Your country, if you allow location when logging fuel: only the country is kept, for six hours, and it is never uploaded. You can say no; fuel prices then use your device's time zone or the country you pick.
  • Saved fuel prices, saved part search results (seven days), half-typed forms and a copy of your garage so the site works without a connection. See Cookies and storage.

Legal basis

Garagebook uses your data to give you the service you asked for when you accepted the invite (GDPR article 6(1)(b)), and to keep the service secure and fair, such as sign-in records and search limits (article 6(1)(f), legitimate interest). Location is only used if you allow it in your browser or on your Mac, and you can turn that off at any time.

If Garagebook is only used by the owner and friends for private purposes, parts of the GDPR may not apply (the "household exemption"). These pages follow the GDPR anyway.

Who can see your garage

Other invited riders can't see your garage. The owner's Admin control shows only your name, when you joined and were last seen, how much space your garage and photos use, and how many part searches you did this week. As the person who manages the database, the owner could technically open stored data, but only does so to fix a problem or when you ask.

How long data is kept

  • Your garage, photos, passkeys and password fingerprint: until you delete your account (Account and sync › Delete account) or the owner does. That removes your account, garage, photos, passkeys and password straight away.
  • Part search records: 32 days. Invite links: until they expire or are used, plus a short time for the owner's overview.
  • Our providers' logs and backups may keep copies for a limited time under their own rules.
  • Data on your device stays until you sign out, clear the site's data, or remove the Mac app and its data. The Mac app keeps your garage on your Mac, also when you don't use online sync.

Your rights

You can ask the owner to see, correct, export or delete your data, or object to how it is used. Much of it you can do yourself: edit anything in the app, export a history report or calendar, and delete your own account under Account and sync › Delete account. You may also complain to the Dutch data protection authority, the Autoriteit Persoonsgegevens, or to the authority in your own EU country.

Security

Sign-in uses passkeys, or a password for riders whose device can't use one: it travels only over HTTPS and is stored only as a salted one-way fingerprint, and after 5 wrong tries the sign-in locks for a while. The database only lets each account read and change its own garage. Connections use HTTPS. No system is perfectly safe; if something goes wrong that affects your data, the owner will tell you.

Children

Garagebook is not meant for anyone under 16.

Changes

If this policy changes, the date at the top changes. Important changes will be mentioned to you by the owner.

Garagebook is a free, private, invite-only project. It is not a company and sells nothing.
